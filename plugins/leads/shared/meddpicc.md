# MEDDPICC rules

## The letters

| Letter | What you must know | Proof that counts |
|---|---|---|
| Metrics | The result the buyer will judge, in numbers, with a date | The buyer says a number or a measure and a date |
| Economic buyer | The person who signs and owns the budget | The buyer names the person who signs. A sponsor who "backs it" is not the signer. |
| Decision criteria | How they choose a vendor | The buyer says what they check |
| Decision process | The steps and dates from now to "yes" | The buyer names the steps or the people in order |
| Paper process | The steps from "yes" to a signed contract: vendor setup, contract type (MSA, SOW), legal, security review, purchase order, invoice terms, and how long each takes | The buyer describes the steps or names who runs them |
| Identify pain | The problem that costs them now | The buyer describes the problem in their own words |
| Champion | A person inside who sells for you when you are not there | The buyer acts for you (books the next call, shares inside facts, tells you the risks) |
| Competition | Who else they talk to, including "do nothing" and "build it in-house" | The buyer names other options |

## Target

A target is a result the buyer will judge, with a number and a date, for example "all 40 staff trained by June". A size or a fact about today ("we have 40 people") is not a target. A goal with a date but no number ("fluent by Q1") is not a target. The core rule of all calls: learn the target before you say any number (a price, a rate, or a size).

## Who signs

The person who asked for the call is often not the economic buyer, mostly when they lead a program or a team and not a budget. Find out who signs.

## States

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| unknown | Nobody said anything about it |
| assumed | We guess it from research, an email, or context. No buyer said it on a call. |
| stated | The buyer said it on a call or in writing. Quote it. |
| validated | A second source confirms it: another person, a document, or an action (for example, the signer joins a call) |

Only buyer words move a letter to `stated`. A seller proposal that the buyer accepts ("That makes sense") is `assumed`, not `stated`, unless the buyer adds facts of their own.

## First calls: "not yet", not "missed"

One call cannot cover all eight letters. On the first call with a lead, a letter that nobody asked about gets the state `unknown` and the note "not yet". Do not grade it as a failure in the table.

This does not excuse the call itself. If the call guide planned a question and the seller did not ask it, the review lists it as a skipped question. The table shows the deal. The review shows the seller.

## Changes between calls

When the deal file already has a table, show each change: old state, new state, and the quote that moved it. A letter can go down, for example when a new person says the budget is not approved.

## Short version for small deals

For a deal under the `small deal` limit in the profile, score only Metrics, Economic buyer, Paper process, and Identify pain.
